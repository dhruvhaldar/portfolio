> _Conducted full-scale aerodynamic simulations of the Mercedes DrivAer model at 130 km/h ($Re = 4.87 \times 10^6$) in a virtual wind tunnel with moving ground boundaries, cross-validating Entropic LBM against ANSYS Discovery and TUM experimental data, and compressing 101 GB simulation data to 0.6 GB (99.4% reduction)._

## Project Overview & Industrial Context

The **DrivAer model**, jointly developed by the Technical University of Munich (TUM) with Audi AG and BMW Group, bridges the gap between generic bluff bodies (like the Ahmed body) and highly complex production passenger vehicles. Featuring realistic underbody styling, engine bay geometry, mirrors, and interchangeable rear upper structures (Notchback, Fastback, Estate), it serves as the global benchmark for passenger car aerodynamics.

In collaboration with **Mercedes**, this project evaluated the full aerodynamic performance and flow field structures of the DrivAer vehicle at highway cruise conditions using SankhyaSutra's proprietary **Entropic Lattice Boltzmann Method (LBM) solver (*Taral*)**, cross-validating the numerical predictions against **ANSYS Discovery / Fluent** and experimental wind tunnel measurements.

## Technical Challenges & Objectives

1. **Complex Separated Wake Dynamics:** Resolving high-Reynolds unsteady shear layers, C-pillar trailing vortices, and underbody diffuser flow separation without empirical RANS eddy-viscosity over-damping.
2. **Realistic Moving Boundaries:** Modeling a full virtual wind tunnel facility with rotating wheels and moving road surfaces at $130 \text{ km/h}$.
3. **Massive Data Handling:** Processing multi-gigabyte transient 3D flow volumes for interactive post-processing and engineering analysis without memory exhaustion.
4. **Cross-Solver Verification:** Correlating Entropic LBM predictions directly with commercial Navier-Stokes solvers (ANSYS Discovery) and experimental wind-tunnel force coefficients.

## Computational Methodology & Physics Setup

### Entropic Lattice Boltzmann Method (LBM)
Traditional CFD methods solve the macroscopic Navier-Stokes conservation equations, relying heavily on empirical turbulence closures (such as $k$-$\epsilon$ or SST $k$-$\omega$) that often over-diffuse detached wake vortices. 

In this study, simulations were executed with **SankhyaSutra Taral**, which solves the discrete Boltzmann transport equation on a structured octree lattice:
- **LES/DNS-like Resolution:** The Entropic formulation ensures strict thermodynamic stability and energy conservation, inherently capturing turbulent scales similarly to Large Eddy Simulation (LES) without empirical calibration parameters.
- **Near-Wall Treatment:** Automatically resolves near-wall shear and boundary layers on curved automotive surfaces using immersed boundary and lattice bounce-back formulations.

### Key Aerodynamic Findings
- **A-Pillar & Mirror Vortex:** Captured the separation bubble originating at the A-pillar and mirror housing, tracking its trajectory along the front side glass.
- **Underbody Diffuser Flow:** Resolved the upward expansion through the rear diffuser, identifying localized vortex bursting responsible for rear lift and induced drag.
- **Rear Slant / C-Pillar Vortices:** Accurately predicted the pair of counter-rotating longitudinal vortices originating along the rear slant edges, matching velocity vector fields extracted from stereo-PIV reference planes.

## Volume Downsampling & Post-Processing Breakthrough

Transient 3D LBM simulations generate immense volume datasets—a single run generated over **101 GB** of volume slice and cell data (`.vtm` / `.vtu`), making interactive visualization in ParaView and GUI tools prohibitively slow.

### The Algorithm
- Developed an automated spatial downsampling algorithm that intelligently filters high-gradient shear layers and boundary cells while downsampling uniform freestream regions.
- Achieved a **99.4% reduction in file size** (compressing the dataset from **101 GB down to 0.6 GB**).
- Enabled instantaneous generation of longitudinal and transverse velocity vector slices, pressure contours, and stream ribbons without loss of coherent turbulent structures.
