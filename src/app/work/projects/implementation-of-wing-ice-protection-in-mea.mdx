> _Demonstrated an 18% to 50% reduction in power consumption and verified a 42.22 kW operating draw during climb by transitioning from conventional bleed-air pneumatic systems to electrothermal wing ice protection._

## Flight Case Study & Engineering Motivation

As part of the aviation industry's Flightpath 2050 environmental goals, More Electric Aircraft (MEA) architectures replace heavy, inefficient pneumatic and hydraulic bleed-air systems with electrical alternatives. In-flight ice accumulation on aerodynamic lifting surfaces poses severe hazards: degrading lift, increasing parasitic drag, altering pitch trim, and risking engine ingestion.

This research analyzes a realistic commercial flight encounter: **SAS Flight 1428 (Stockholm Arlanda ARN to Copenhagen CPN)** operated by an **Airbus A320neo**, evaluating in-flight icing on an Airbus wing section (NACA 24012 airfoil profile) during climb and descent phases.

## Comparative Architectures: P-WIPS vs. E-WIPS

1. **Pneumatic Wing Ice Protection System (P-WIPS):** Conventional architecture extracting high-pressure, high-temperature bleed air from engine compressors and the Auxiliary Power Unit (APU). Air is routed through internal **Piccolo tubes** and discharged against the inner leading-edge skin, transferring heat via Conjugate Heat Transfer (CHT) before exhausting overboard.
2. **Electrothermal Wing Ice Protection System (E-WIPS):** Modern MEA architecture utilizing multi-zone electrothermal heating mats embedded along the wing leading edge (parting strips and shedding zones). The system applies transient heat pulses to break the ice-surface adhesive bond, shedding accretion aerodynamically with minimal thermal energy waste.

## Multi-Physics Simulation Methodology

A decoupled, iterative "divide-and-conquer" simulation framework was formulated using **ANSYS FENSAP-ICE**:

1. **Aerodynamic Flow Solution (FENSAP):** Solved compressible Navier-Stokes momentum and energy equations with Spalart-Allmaras and $k$-$\omega$ SST turbulence models, establishing wall distance fields and boundary layer profiles on anisotropic OPTIGRID meshes.
2. **Droplet Impingement & Trajectory (DROP3D):** Computed Eulerian droplet trajectories and local collection efficiency ($\beta$) across seven droplet diameter bins adhering to a **Langmuir D distribution** with a Median Volume Diameter (MVD) of $20\ \mu\text{m}$.
3. **Phase-Change Thermodynamics & Accretion (ICE3D):** Evaluated mass deficit, water film runback, and thermodynamic freezing regimes to compute accreted ice geometry and total accreted mass.
4. **Internal Aerothermal Heating (ANSYS Fluent):** Simulated conjugate internal Piccolo tube turbulent airflow and outer skin thermal dissipation.

### Key Validation Outcomes

- **Ice Accretion Profile Correlation:** The ICE3D prediction accurately replicated the experimental stagnation horn angle and upper/lower surface impingement cutoff points observed in the NASA Lewis IRT facility.
- **Quantitative Mass Conservation:** Predicted total ice mass of **0.429 kg** matched the measured experimental mass within experimental uncertainty.
- **Rime vs. Glaze Regimes:** Accurately captured instantaneous rime freezing at low temperatures and the runback liquid film dynamics characteristic of glaze icing.

## Workflow Automation & Technical Contributions

- **Geometry & Mesh Scripting:** Developed automated IronPython pre-processing scripts within ANSYS SpaceClaim to parameterize airfoil profiles, chord divisions, and slat cross-sections.
- **Turbulence Model Calibration:** Evaluated Spalart-Allmaras and $k$-$\omega$ SST models against high-gradient leading-edge thermal boundary layer data.
- **Energy Optimization Findings:** Electrothermal WIPS achieved an estimated **18% to 50% power reduction** compared to bleed-air pneumatic systems, directly supporting lower thrust specific fuel consumption (TSFC) and reduced greenhouse gas emissions.
